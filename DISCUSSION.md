# Discussion and bibliographic notes

## How to read the two results

**Spiral Stretch.** As deposited, the data show a large and consistent advantage for adding the Spiral Stretch
technique: about 11 SPADI points, 1.4 NPRS points and 13 to 14 degrees of flexion and abduction more improvement
than physiotherapy alone over 4 weeks. Taken at face value the SPADI difference is in the range often quoted as
clinically important for SPADI (roughly 8 to 13 points; Roy, MacDermid and Woodhouse 2009) and the pain difference
is below the 2 point change often used for NPRS (Farrar et al. 2001). Two things make face value hard to accept:

1. The standardised effects (Hedges g 1.2 to 2.9) are far larger than add-on physiotherapy techniques usually show,
   and they are large because the change scores barely vary. Every one of 24 people improved on every outcome;
   pain dropped by exactly 5 points in 9 of 12 treated people and by exactly 3 in 8 of 12 controls; after treatment
   the groups' flexion and abduction values do not overlap. Real goniometry and pain ratings are usually noisier.
2. Basic consistency checks fail in more than half of the rows (BMI) and in one derived score (flexion).

The noise stress test turns this into a number: if each person's pain change had an extra random error with an SD
of 1.5 points, about half of the simulated trials would no longer show a pain difference. The finding is therefore
only as reliable as the recorded regularity. It cannot be confirmed without the authors' raw case records, which
are not public.

**Magnetotherapy.** Both groups improved by similar large amounts over 2 weeks (about 30 SPADI points and 3.5 NPRS
points), and the between group differences are all close to zero with intervals that include zero. With 18 people
per group the intervals are still wide (for SPADI, about 4 points either way), so the data neither show nor rule
out a small added effect. One of 24 sensitivity contrasts excludes zero (adduction, -2.2 degrees, favouring
Group 2, after removing repeated SPADI pairs); with 24 looks this is the rate expected by chance and it is not
interpreted. Large improvements in both arms over only 2 weeks, with every person improving on every
outcome, again point to a regular pattern that would be unusual in clinical data; natural history, regression to
the mean and co-interventions cannot be separated from treatment without an untreated arm.

## Why the trials are not pooled

They come from the same department but test different add-ons (a manual stretching technique vs pulsed
electromagnetic fields), at different doses and durations, with different outcome sets and coding. Pooling would
average two different questions, and with only two small single centre deposits a pooled number would add false
precision, not evidence. The side by side figures show the contrast in patterns instead.

## Limits

* Small samples (24 and 36), single centre, no follow up beyond the end of treatment.
* No results paper for either trial, so there is nothing to replicate or compare with.
* No codebook for the magnetotherapy file; group and sex codes are undocumented.
* The Spiral Stretch protocol restricts inclusion to diabetic patients; the data cannot confirm this.
* Ethics and registration of the magnetotherapy trial are unverified.
* Bootstrap percentile intervals with 12 per group can be too narrow; Welch intervals are shown alongside and agree.

## What would resolve the questions

* A results paper or a fuller deposit (raw goniometry, per session records, CONSORT flow) for each trial.
* A codebook for `NEW.xlsx`.
* Confirmation of the magnetotherapy ethics approval and registration.
